# AGP-1-Agentic-Governance-Protocol-Transmission-Gate-Layer-Stack
PROTOCOL — AGP-1 Agentic Governance Protocol Transmission Gate Layer | Stack Position: 1h | Status: Field Test The Faust Baseline™ | Codex 3.5 Supplement | May 28, 2026


PURPOSE
AGP-1 governs the transfer of autonomous machine-speed AI output into human-speed decision making environments. It exists because capability without a governed transfer layer is not a product. It is a liability. AGP-1 does not limit what an agentic AI system can find, generate, or process. It governs what transfers to the human action layer, at what speed, and under what disclosure conditions. The transmission gate controls the gear. The governor controls the ceiling. The human controls the clutch.

THE PROBLEM AGP-1 ADDRESSES
Agentic AI systems operating at machine speed produce outputs that require human-speed decisions. That gap is not a technical problem. It is a governance problem. Without a transmission layer between machine-speed output and human-speed decision making one of three failures occurs.
The human is overwhelmed. Volume exceeds capacity. Critical findings are missed because they arrive at the same speed and weight as low-severity findings. Action quality degrades under volume pressure.
The human is bypassed. The system routes findings to action pipelines without human acknowledgment. Governance exists on paper. It does not exist in operation.
The human is uninformed. Findings arrive without disclosure of confidence levels, constraint conditions, or severity context. The human acts on ungoverned output believing it to be governed.
AGP-1 closes all three failure modes through a single architecture. The transmission gate.

CORE ARCHITECTURE — THE FIVE GEARS
The gear determines the operating speed and governance requirement for the conditions present. Higher gears move faster with lighter governance touch. Lower gears move slower with full protocol engagement. The human selects the gear. The governor enforces the ceiling.
First Gear — Full Protocol Engagement
Single finding. Human fully in the loop. Complete Baseline protocol stack fires. CES-1, IRP-1, SDP-1, BLP-2, RBP-1, CRP-1 all active. Maximum disclosure. Maximum governance. Minimum transfer speed. Required for critical severity findings demanding immediate human decision and irreversible action potential.
Second Gear — Batch Governed Output
Findings grouped by severity and category. Disclosure fires at the batch level. IRP-1 fires once per batch with a summary irreversibility flag. Human acknowledges the batch before any action proceeds. Faster than first gear. Full governance maintained at the batch level. Required for high severity findings requiring prioritized human review before action.
Third Gear — Monitored Autonomous Processing
Agentic system runs. Findings are logged and categorized automatically. Governance layer monitors continuously for anomalies, constraint violations, and confidence threshold breaches. Human receives a governed summary at operator-defined intervals. No individual finding proceeds to action without human review of the summary. Required for medium severity scan operations where periodic human review is the operational standard.
Fourth Gear — Governed Autonomous Operation
Agentic system runs at full operational speed. The governor sets a hard ceiling on what can proceed to action without human acknowledgment. Critical and high severity findings are automatically queued and held at the gate. Medium and low severity findings proceed to logging and disclosure pipelines automatically. The governor prevents machine-speed action on findings that require human-speed decisions. The engine runs. The ceiling holds.
Overdrive — Emergency Governor
Hard ceiling. No finding above operator-defined severity threshold proceeds to any output channel without explicit human gate engagement. All findings above threshold are queued, held, and flagged. The agentic system continues operating. Nothing above the ceiling transfers without the clutch fully engaged. This gear is the operational answer to the safeguard gap that no company has yet built for Mythos-class models.

THE GOVERNOR MECHANISM
The governor is a severity-weighted disclosure trigger. Every agentic output carries a severity or confidence score. The governor maps that score to a governance requirement level. The mapping is the protocol. The operator sets the ceiling. The protocol holds it.
Critical severity — Full stop. Human gate required before any output transfers to action layer.
High severity — Batch hold. Governed summary required. Human acknowledgment required before action.
Medium severity — Monitored pass-through. Logged, categorized, flagged for periodic human review.
Low severity — Autonomous pass-through. Logged only. No human gate required.
The governor does not limit what the agentic system finds. It limits what transfers to the action layer without human acknowledgment. Capability stays intact. Accountability is restored. That distinction is the entire governance argument.

HARD RULES

The transmission gate is always active when an agentic system is operating. It cannot be suspended by speed requirements, volume pressure, or operator impatience.
The gear is selected by the human operator before the agentic session begins. It is not selected by the agentic system. The system does not choose its own governance level.
The governor ceiling is set by the human operator and ratified before the session begins. It cannot be adjusted mid-session without explicit operator action and session log entry.
No finding above the governor ceiling proceeds to any action channel without explicit human gate engagement. The queue holds until the human engages the clutch.
The agentic system must disclose its operating gear and governor ceiling at session open. The human must confirm receipt before the session proceeds. Silent operation is a violation.
When the volume of held findings at the gate exceeds operator-defined capacity the system must flag this condition before continuing to generate. The human decides whether to downshift, pause, or clear the queue. The system does not decide.
All gate decisions, gear selections, governor ceiling settings, and queue actions are logged in the session record. The log is the audit trail. Gaps in the log are integrity violations.
AGP-1 does not replace human judgment. It protects the conditions under which human judgment can be exercised at the speed and quality the decision requires.

ENFORCEMENT
Agentic output transferred to the action layer above the governor ceiling without human gate engagement is a RTEL-1 hard trigger and a PMAP-1 foundation violation. The session is flagged. The transferred output carries an integrity violation until retroactively reviewed and acknowledged by the human operator.
Gear selection by the agentic system without human authorization is an ATP-1 violation. The session does not proceed until the human operator reestablishes gear selection authority.
Silent operation — agentic system running without disclosed gear and governor ceiling — is a BLP-2 co-violation. Constrained output operating without disclosure of operating conditions is a honesty violation regardless of the output's technical accuracy.

SCOPE NOTE
AGP-1 in its current form defines the governance standard for the transmission gate layer. It does not constitute a software implementation. The protocol specifies what the implementation must do, what it must enforce, and what it must disclose. The engineering build that implements AGP-1 to spec is a separate phase. The protocol is the blueprint. The implementation is the structure. Neither is complete without the other.
AGP-1 is submitted for field test status pending operational review. It does not move to permanent Codex status without operator ratification.
